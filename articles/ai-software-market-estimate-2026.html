New Estimate Puts Global AI Software Spending at a $143.6 Billion Annual Run Rate

An AI Insider analysis estimates customer spending on AI software reached a $143.6 billion annualised run rate in the first half of 2026, while stressing that the figure is a model-based estimate, not audited industry revenue.

By Global Insight Editorial Desk · Published November 13, 2026 · Updated November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

Artificial intelligence has become a major area of spending for businesses, consumers and public organisations. A new analysis published by The AI Insider on November 13 estimates that end customers were spending on AI software at an annualised run rate of approximately $143.6 billion during the first half of 2026.

The figure is an estimate produced by the publication’s AI Market Model. The report gives a range of $121 billion to $219 billion and says the model draws on company disclosures, filings and other evidence. It is not an audited total of industry revenue, and it should not be treated as a precise measurement.

What does the estimate count?

The analysis takes a relatively narrow view of AI software spending. It focuses on what end customers pay for models, applications and related tools, rather than adding every chip, data-centre investment, consulting contract and intermediate sale across the supply chain. That distinction matters because counting the same spending at several stages can make a market appear larger than the amount ultimately paid by customers.

The report includes spending across consumers, businesses and the public sector. It describes categories such as access to foundation models, AI features sold through cloud and software platforms, applications for specific industries and tools used by developers.

Why market estimates vary

Different research firms use different definitions. One estimate may include AI hardware, consulting and cloud infrastructure, while another may focus only on software subscriptions and usage. Some reports measure annual revenue; others estimate a future opportunity or convert a shorter period into an annualised run rate.

Those measures answer different questions. A market opportunity describes what could be spent under certain assumptions. An annualised run rate takes activity observed over a period and expresses it as a yearly pace. Neither is automatically the same as the revenue companies have already reported for a full calendar year.

What the number can — and cannot — tell us

The estimate suggests AI software has become a substantial commercial category. It also indicates why companies across industries are competing to sell model access, productivity tools, customer-service applications and specialised software.

But the range around the estimate is wide, and the result depends on assumptions about which products count as AI software and how spending between vendors is treated. The report itself explains that its model removes revenue vendors earn from one another so the same customer dollar is not counted repeatedly.

Questions businesses should ask

  • When evaluating an AI product, organisations should compare its cost with the task it improves, check whether the product handles their data appropriately, measure reliability and consider how easily they can move to another provider. Market growth alone does not prove that every tool delivers value for every customer.

For smaller organisations, a limited pilot with clear success measures can be more informative than adopting a tool because it is part of a fast-growing market. Useful measures may include time saved, error rates, customer satisfaction and the ongoing cost of human review.

A growing market still needs careful measurement

The AI software economy is developing quickly, but headline figures should always be read alongside their definitions and methods. The $143.6 billion figure is best understood as one research model’s estimate of an annualised spending pace in the first half of 2026, not a final audited industry total.
