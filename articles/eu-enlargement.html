European Union Enlargement: How New Countries Can Join the EU

Global Insight Editorial Team · October 19, 2026 · 6 min read

The European Union (EU) is a political and economic union of European countries that cooperate on trade, shared rules and a range of regional policies. Its membership has expanded over time, and further enlargement remains an important issue in European politics.

What does EU enlargement mean?

Enlargement is the process through which a European country can become a member of the European Union. Membership is not automatic: a country must meet established conditions, align its laws with EU requirements and complete negotiations with existing member states.

Requirements for membership

Candidate countries are expected to demonstrate stable democratic institutions, the rule of law, respect for human rights and protection of minorities. They must also have a functioning market economy and the ability to implement the obligations associated with EU membership. These requirements are commonly associated with the Copenhagen criteria.

How the accession process works

The process can involve applying for membership, receiving candidate status, opening negotiations and working through different policy chapters. Progress is assessed against EU rules and standards. The process can take years, and decisions require agreement from existing member states under the applicable procedures.

Potential economic benefits

Membership can provide access to the EU single market, which supports the movement of goods, services, capital and people under common rules. It may also encourage investment, improve regulatory cooperation and create opportunities for businesses. However, the benefits depend on economic conditions, national policies and how effectively reforms are implemented.

Political and economic challenges

Accession can require extensive legal reforms, stronger institutions and changes to national policies. Existing EU members must also consider how enlargement could affect the union's budget, decision-making, agriculture, labour markets and regional development. Disagreements between governments can slow the process.

Does enlargement happen on a fixed timetable?

No. There is no automatic date by which a candidate country becomes an EU member. The timeline depends on reform progress, negotiations and the required political approvals. Announcements about possible future membership should therefore be distinguished from formal decisions made by EU institutions and member states.

What to watch next

Readers following EU enlargement should look for official announcements on candidate status, negotiation milestones, progress reports and decisions by the Council of the European Union. These sources help distinguish confirmed developments from political proposals or speculation about future membership.
