Microsoft 365 Outage Disrupts Outlook and Teams: What Users Need to Know

A November 13, 2026 service disruption affected Outlook, Teams and other Microsoft 365 services. Here is what is confirmed, who was affected and what users should check.

By Global Insight Editorial Desk · Published November 13, 2026 · Updated November 13, 2026 · 5 min read

A widespread disruption to Microsoft 365 services left some people and organisations unable to use familiar workplace tools on November 12 and 13, 2026. Reports described problems with Outlook email, Microsoft Teams and other connected services, highlighting how much everyday communication now depends on a small number of cloud platforms.

Which services were affected?

Coverage of the incident listed Outlook.com, OneDrive, OneNote, Microsoft To Do, Teams and Microsoft 365 for the web among the services experiencing problems. The impact was not identical for every user: some people reported trouble opening inboxes or sending messages, while others encountered problems with calendars, collaboration features or connected business workflows.

The disruption was reported across several regions and sectors. The Guardian’s live coverage also described effects on organisations outside the United States, including businesses and service providers in Asia and Australia. The precise experience depended on the service, region and organisation.

What did Microsoft say about the cause?

During the response, Microsoft said it was working to restore service and described efforts involving capacity, traffic management and servers that were not responding as expected. The company also said there was no indication at that point that the incident was the result of malicious cyber activity. That distinction matters: an outage is not, by itself, evidence of a cyberattack.

Service investigations can change as engineers gather more information. Readers should therefore treat early explanations as provisional and consult Microsoft’s own service-health notices for the latest confirmed status.

Why a cloud outage can have a wide impact

Cloud services connect email, calendars, file storage, identity systems and business applications. When one part of that infrastructure becomes unavailable, the effects can spread to organisations that use different products but rely on the same underlying platform. A service interruption can therefore affect more than the app that first appears to be offline.

The incident is also a reminder that digital resilience is a practical business issue. Organisations can reduce disruption by maintaining clear incident communication plans, keeping essential contact details accessible and ensuring employees know which approved alternatives to use when a primary service is unavailable.

What should users do?

  • Use Microsoft’s official service-health page or your organisation’s IT team for current status information. Avoid repeatedly changing passwords or reinstalling applications unless official support guidance recommends it. If a message claims to offer an urgent outage fix, verify it through a trusted channel before opening links or attachments.

As services recover, some features may return at different times. A successful sign-in does not necessarily mean every connected feature is fully restored.

The bigger lesson

The November disruption shows how important reliability has become for cloud-based communication. Cloud platforms offer convenience and scale, but organisations still need sensible continuity plans and transparent updates when services fail.
